Workforce age & gender scorecard

Each company's score blends how well it employs people 40 and over (age) with how close it is to gender parity. Ages are estimated from bachelor's graduation year (age 21 at graduation), or first job start year if there's no degree. Gender is inferred from first names using US Social Security birth records, and scored twice: across all staff and within STEM roles.

Load employee data

CSV with one row per employee. Needed: company plus a name. Useful: ticker, job title (for the STEM indicator), bachelor's graduation year, first job start year/date, pronouns, self-reported gender. Everything runs in your browser — nothing is uploaded.

Scoring settings

Names between the two thresholds (Taylor, Casey, Jordan, Jessie…) count as undetermined unless pronouns, a self-reported gender, an honorific (Ms./Mr.) or an unambiguous middle name settles it. The 55+ default is BLS's share of employed people aged 55 and over (2019); the 40+ default is a placeholder — set it to your peer benchmark.

How the score works

  • Age. Estimated age = 21 + (as-of year − bachelor's graduation year). With no graduation year, the first job start year stands in for age 21. Estimates under 18 or over 85 are discarded. Age score = average of (share 40+ ÷ benchmark) and (share 55+ ÷ benchmark), each capped at 100.
  • Gender. Order of evidence: self-reported gender → pronouns (she/he/they) → honorific → first name → middle name. A name counts as female or male only if US births 1940–2006 carrying it were at or beyond your thresholds; non-US names not in SSA data fall back to an international name dictionary. Gender score = 100 × (non-male share ÷ 50%), capped at 100, so parity scores 100.
  • STEM. A role counts as STEM when its job title matches technical keywords (engineer, developer, data scientist, security analyst, DevOps, IT, scientist, CTO/CISO…) and isn't a business role (sales, recruiting, marketing, finance, HR, legal — though "sales engineer" stays STEM). An optional STEM column (yes/no) overrides the title match. STEM score uses the same parity formula as overall gender, applied only to STEM staff; ⚠ marks companies with fewer STEM staff of known gender than your confidence minimum.
  • DEI score = weighted average of the three parts. A company with no title data is scored on the parts it has. Shares use only employees whose age or gender could be determined; coverage columns show how many that is.
  • Known biases. Graduation-year ages run young for people who finished college late or changed careers, and first-job years run old for people who started working before 21. Name inference under-counts women with less common or non-Western names (more of them land in "undetermined") and cannot see non-binary people unless pronouns are listed. Treat the score as a screening signal, not a finding about any individual.
